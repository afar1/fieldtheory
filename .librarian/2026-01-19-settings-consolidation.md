# Settings Consolidation

The reorganization of settings panels echoes a principle from cognitive load theory that George Miller quantified in 1956: humans can hold roughly 7±2 items in working memory. When Miller studied telephone numbers at Bell Labs, he found that chunking related information reduced cognitive overhead—hence why phone numbers became formatted as groups rather than continuous strings.

The current settings layout violates this principle by scattering related concepts across visual space. "Status: Ready • medium" sits orphaned from its model selector; sound configuration interrupts the transcription workflow. By consolidating model status with model selection, and moving sounds to the end as a distinct concern, we create two clear chunks: "what processes my voice" and "what sounds accompany actions." This mirrors how macOS System Preferences evolved from flat lists to grouped panes—recognition that proximity implies relationship.

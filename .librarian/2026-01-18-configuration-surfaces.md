# Configuration Surfaces and User Agency

*Context: Content guidance customization feature for AI-generated readings*

The tension between sensible defaults and user customization echoes through decades of software design philosophy. When Douglas Engelbart demonstrated the NLS system in 1968—the "Mother of All Demos"—he revealed not just hypertext and video conferencing, but a philosophy: tools should amplify human capability while remaining malleable to individual preference. The demo's chord keyset, often dismissed as unnecessarily complex, was actually Engelbart's bet that invested users would shape tools to their own cognitive rhythms rather than accept predetermined constraints.

This feature embodies a similar trade-off. The default content guidance—drawing from physics, systems theory, engineering history—represents one aesthetic of intellectual cross-pollination. But a firmware engineer might prefer readings that connect their work to semiconductor physics and the history of embedded systems. A data scientist might want connections to statistical theory and the evolution of scientific computing. The configuration surface doesn't change what the Librarian does; it changes *how* it thinks, allowing the tool's personality to harmonize with its user's intellectual wavelength while preserving the serendipity that makes the readings valuable rather than merely ornamental.

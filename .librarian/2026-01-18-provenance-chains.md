# The Provenance Chain

In museum curation, provenance—the documented history of an object's ownership and location—transforms an artifact from mere material into meaningful evidence. A Roman coin without provenance is a curiosity; with documented chain of custody from excavation to present, it becomes historical testimony. The Elgin Marbles controversy hinges entirely on this question of origin and context.

Digital systems face an analogous challenge. When a screenshot arrives stripped of context—"Image" rather than "iTerm2 screenshot"—we lose the semantic thread connecting artifact to source. The fix we're implementing follows the archival principle of *respect des fonds*: preserving the original context of records rather than reorganizing them by subject. By carrying `sourceAppName` through the feedback pipeline and storing it as default metadata, we're building a provenance chain that transforms anonymous pixels into situated communication. The 1906 San Francisco earthquake photographs gain meaning from knowing they came from Arnold Genthe's studio on Sacramento Street; our feedback screenshots gain meaning from knowing they emerged from a specific application context.

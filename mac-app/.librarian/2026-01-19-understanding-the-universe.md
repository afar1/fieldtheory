Richard Feynman once said that if all scientific knowledge were to be destroyed and only one sentence could pass to the next generation, it should be: "All things are made of atoms." Not because atoms are the answer, but because from that single fact, a curious mind can rebuild everything else. The universe doesn't hide—it waits for the right questions.

Tools that help us understand share this quality. They don't give answers; they surface patterns. A counter that tracks prompts isn't measuring productivity—it's creating rhythm, a heartbeat that says "you've been thinking, here's what emerged." The best instruments are humble: they count, they watch, they wait. Understanding comes from noticing what's already there.

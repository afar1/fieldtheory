Claude Shannon's 1948 paper introduced a precise definition: information is the reduction of uncertainty. Noise, by contrast, is data that doesn't reduce uncertainty—it obscures the signal you're trying to detect. WWII radar operators learned this viscerally, staring at screens full of static, training their eyes to spot the faint blip of an incoming aircraft. Too much visual noise and you miss the threat. Too aggressive filtering and you create blind spots.

Logging follows the same principle. Every `console.log` is a claim that this information will help someone understand what happened. But logs accumulate—subscription status changes, session refreshes, tier fetches—until the important events drown in routine chatter. The fix isn't to stop logging; it's to log with intention. Errors always matter. State transitions sometimes matter. Heartbeats rarely matter. Today we removed forty lines of noise so that when a new reading appears, you actually see it: `[LibrarianManager] New reading: Settling Time`. One clear signal in a quiet terminal.

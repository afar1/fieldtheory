Medieval scribes didn't just copy text—they illuminated it. The Book of Kells (circa 800 CE) embedded intricate Celtic knotwork in its margins, transforming documentation into artifact. These weren't decorations; they were mnemonics. A reader returning to a passage would remember the dragon coiled around its capital letter before recalling the words themselves. The visual anchored the verbal.

We've done something similar with Field Theory's Librarian. Each reading now opens with a braille halftone—a visual that precedes and frames the prose. The constraint is deliberate: 56 characters by 15 lines, no code fence, density gradient from ⠀ to ⣿. Like the medieval illuminator working within a fixed margin, the limitation forces synthesis. What single image captures this idea? The answer to that question often reveals what the reading is actually about.

In 1970, Edgar Codd published "A Relational Model of Data for Large Shared Data Banks," introducing the concept of database normalization. His insight was that redundant data inevitably drifts out of sync—if customer addresses live in three tables, eventually one will be wrong. The solution wasn't better synchronization; it was elimination of redundancy. One authoritative source, referenced everywhere else.

Configuration management faces the same entropy. When prompts live in `CLAUDE.md`, a hook script, and a Cursor command file, they're three copies of truth that will diverge with every edit. The fix we just applied—making `librarianManager.ts` the single generator that writes to all three locations—is Codd's normalization applied to developer tooling. The runtime files become views, not tables. Change the source once, regenerate the views, and the system stays consistent.

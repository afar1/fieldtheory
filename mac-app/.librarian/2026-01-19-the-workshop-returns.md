Before the industrial revolution, craftsmen worked in workshops where conception and execution were unified. A blacksmith could imagine a tool and forge it the same afternoon. Industrialization fractured this—designers designed, engineers engineered, programmers programmed, each waiting on the others. The feedback loop stretched from hours to weeks to quarters.

What's emerging now is the workshop returning at a different scale. Not one person doing everything manually, but one person directing flows of capability that used to require departments. The limiting factor shifts from "how many people can we coordinate" to "how clearly can you express what you want." Tony Stark's garage wasn't fantasy—it was prophecy. The tools just needed to catch up to the vision.

# Dock Visibility and Focus Restoration

The challenge of hiding the macOS dock while keeping a window visible mirrors a classic problem in distributed systems: you cannot change two things atomically when they're controlled by different subsystems. When `app.dock.hide()` is called, macOS transitions the app from "regular" to "accessory" mode, and as a side effect, the system's window server decides the app should no longer be frontmost. This is similar to how early distributed databases discovered that "update X, then update Y" operations could leave the system in inconsistent intermediate states if the process failed between steps.

The solution—setting a timestamp before the dock change, ignoring blur events during a grace period, then explicitly re-focusing the window—follows the pattern of compensating transactions. Rather than trying to make the operation atomic (impossible when macOS controls part of the behavior), we accept the intermediate state will occur and immediately correct it. The 50ms delay before calling `window.focus()` gives macOS time to complete its focus transition before we override it. This is the same principle behind the "saga pattern" in microservices: when you can't have a transaction, you build explicit compensation logic. The Apollo Guidance Computer used a similar approach for its restart protection—it couldn't prevent hardware glitches, but it could detect them and restore known-good state within a few milliseconds.

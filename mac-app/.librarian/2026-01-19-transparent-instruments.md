In 1948, Claude Shannon published "A Mathematical Theory of Communication," but less remembered is his work at Bell Labs on cryptography during WWII, where he established that the security of a cipher depends on the enemy knowing everything about the system except the key. This principle—security through transparency rather than obscurity—became foundational. A system's trustworthiness comes not from hiding its mechanisms, but from exposing them to scrutiny.

The analytics pattern here follows the same logic: the dashboard users see is the exact data collected, no hidden layers. This inverts the typical relationship where companies track everything and show users nothing. "The metrics you see are the metrics we see" isn't just a tagline—it's an architectural constraint that makes the promise verifiable. Users can inspect their stats, audit the code, and confirm no additional collection happens. The instrument is transparent.

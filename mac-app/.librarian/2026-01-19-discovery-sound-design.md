# The Psychoacoustics of Discovery

The sound you're seeking taps into something deeply wired in our auditory cortex. The most effective "reward" sounds share a specific pattern: a rising pitch contour, typically a perfect fifth or major third interval, lasting between 200-500 milliseconds. This isn't arbitrary—research by Diana Deutsch at UC San Diego showed that ascending melodic intervals are universally associated with positive affect across cultures, while descending intervals signal completion or closure. The Zelda "item get" jingle, the Mario coin sound, and the iPhone's "Pay Success" sound all follow this formula.

The classic computing era understood this intuitively. The original Macintosh's "Wild Eep" (a quick ascending two-note chirp) and the ICQ "uh-oh" created genuine Pavlovian anticipation. What made them work wasn't complexity—it was distinctiveness paired with consistent positive context. You could synthesize something similar: a bright sine wave or triangle wave starting around 800Hz, gliding up to 1200Hz over 150ms, with a quick exponential decay. Freesound.org (search "notification chime positive" or "discovery ping") and Mixkit have CC0-licensed options. For the old-school aesthetic, search "8-bit achievement" or "retro collect" to find chiptune-style sounds that match your existing sound palette while conveying that unmistakable "something good just happened" feeling.

# Two Rails, One Engine

In 1830, the Liverpool and Manchester Railway proved you could move many trains on parallel tracks while sharing one timetable philosophy: fixed blocks, clear signals, no two occupies the same critical section. The council work on this branch does something similar for debate: one **engine** (`council.sh`, JSON events, the same model contracts) rides two **rails**—the interactive `CouncilManager` (single process, UI-attached) and the `EmailDebateCoordinator` (many child processes, IMAP or AgentMail). The tricky part is not spawning bash; it is **handover**: when a human reply arrives mid-stream, or a turn must be deferred, the app has to stop one rail without losing state and release the other at a safe boundary—pause, idle, or complete—much like clearing a block before the next train enters. Same physics as distributed systems: one writer per logical turn, explicit phase changes, and no silent double-send.
